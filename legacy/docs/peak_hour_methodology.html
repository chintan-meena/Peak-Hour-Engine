Northern Region · Hydro Peak Hours

A reproducible method for declaring monthly peak hours

The window is chosen by an explicit, auditable rule over published data, so any declaration can be re-derived and defended after the fact. Every design choice below is stated with the evidence that motivated it.

§1

What is being decided

For each calendar month, one 3-hour window is declared in advance as peak hours. The declaration is issued roughly ten days before the month begins, so it must be made from information available at that time — nothing from the month itself.

The day is divided into 96 blocks of 15 minutes, numbered 1–96 from 00:00. A declaration is a set of 12 blocks, expressed as clock ranges such as 18:45–21:45 or 06:30–07:45, 17:30–19:15.

§2

Constraints

These are fixed by physics and practice, not chosen by the method:

  • Exactly 12 blocks (3 hours). Hydro storage is limited; the window cannot be widened to capture more. This is a hard cap, not a tunable parameter.
  • One continuous window, or two segments. Each segment must be at least 4 blocks (1 hour). Observed declarations use splits of (6,6), (5,7) and (4,8).
  • Within the calendar day. No window crosses midnight.
§3

Objective

Peak hours should mark the blocks where the system is under the most stress. The method scores each block by the product of net load and real-time market price, and selects the admissible window with the greatest total.

declare = argmaxW ∈ admissible   Σb ∈ W  NetLoad(b) × RTM(b)

Net load — demand less solar and wind — is what dispatchable generation must actually serve. RTM price is the market's own scarcity signal. The product captures both how much is needed and how scarce it is at that moment; either alone is weaker. Both series are published and independently verifiable, so the score can be recomputed by anyone.

One objective produces the right seasonal behaviour with no seasonal rule.

Winter mornings are genuinely expensive — fog suppresses solar while demand climbs — so they score well and a morning segment appears. Summer mornings are cheaper than the rest of the day, because solar ramps hard, so no morning segment appears. Nothing in the method knows what a season is.

§4

Information set

Block profiles are built from the same calendar month in the two preceding years — for September 2026, that is September 2025 and September 2024. The target month contributes nothing.

Adjacent months are deliberately excluded, and this matters more than it first appears. Peak timing tracks sunset: winter net load peaks at block 76 (18:45), summer at block 90 (22:15). Averaging May and June into a July profile drags the declared window one to two hours early.

Measured effect of the information set

Out-of-sample over 19 months, frequency stress captured: M−12, M−1, M−2 → 57.9%  ·  M−12 → 67.4%  ·  M−12, M−24 → 67.6%. Including recent months makes the declaration measurably worse.

§5

Selection

Every admissible window is enumerated and scored; the highest total is declared. The search is exhaustive, so there is no optimiser to inspect and no starting point to justify — the same inputs always yield the same window.

The method has no fitted parameters. There are no weights to tune and therefore nothing to overfit. This was a deliberate choice: a weighted scheme combining net-load level, ramp and price was tested and fitted on 18 months, and it performed worse out of sample than the unweighted objective (82.8% against 98.5% value captured). With samples this small, added parameters cost more than they return.

§7

Independent validation

Value capture is computed from the same quantity the method maximises, so it cannot confirm the method on its own. Grid frequency provides a check that is entirely outside the objective: it appears nowhere in the score, and it cannot be forecast at declaration time.

Frequency falls when demand outruns generation. If a declared window genuinely marks system stress, frequency inside it should sit below the rest of the day. Across the study window the fraction of minutes below 49.90 Hz is 0.084 in winter mornings and 0.068 in winter evenings, against 0.058 for the rest of the day.

Used this way, frequency answers a question the objective cannot: if frequency stays depressed through the declared window even with hydro despatched, demand was genuinely large — the window found real scarcity rather than a pricing artefact.

§8

Evidence

37 months were replayed from history alone, then scored against what actually happened. Ex-post optimum is the best window the constraints allow, computed with perfect hindsight — the ceiling, not an achievable target.

§9

Limitations

Stated plainly, because a defensible method is one whose weak points are already known.

  • Value capture is partly self-referential. Selecting on historical net load × RTM and scoring on realised net load × RTM share an objective. The comparison is honest out-of-sample, but the frequency column is the independent one and its margins are narrower.
  • Climatology assumes persistence. The method presumes the prior two years resemble the coming month. A structural break — a tariff change, a large capacity addition, a market-coupling change — would not be anticipated.
  • Frequency is all-India. One synchronous grid means frequency reflects national rather than regional scarcity, so it corroborates but cannot localise.
  • Morning segments are an outcome, not a guarantee. They emerge when morning blocks score well, which happened in 13 of 18 winter months. If a morning segment is required as policy, it must be imposed as a constraint rather than left to the objective.
  • Single region, 37 months. Results are Northern Region only, and the backtest is short relative to the seasonal cycles it spans.

Reproduction

Every input is a published series: NR net load from SCADA, RTM prices from the exchange, grid frequency from SCADA. Given a target month, the declaration is a deterministic function of the two prior same-month profiles — no seeds, no fitted weights, no manual adjustment. Re-running the procedure on the same inputs reproduces the same window exactly.
